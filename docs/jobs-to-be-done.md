# Jobs to Be Done: every Far Cooler surface

Generated by `/office-hours` on 2026-08-20.
Scope: the Apple surfaces — Mac, phone, Live Activity, phone widgets, watch app,
watch complication. Android mirrors the phone and inherits its jobs; the CLI
serves a different person in a different place and is out of scope here.

Companion to [`farcooler-design.md`](farcooler-design.md), which says what the
*product* is for. This says what each *surface* is for, which that document has
never said.

## Why this document exists

Far Cooler has six user-facing Apple surfaces and, until now, one articulated
job between them — the watch's, written last week. Everything else accumulated
features against no stated boundary. That is how a Mac app grows a Layout menu
and a phone app grows a diff viewer without anyone having decided that either
belongs there.

The cost is not theoretical. "Audit findings" below records six things this
exercise turned up, including a job that is implemented three times and named
zero times, and a set of glance surfaces that cannot act.

## Method

**Jobs come from situations, not from features.** The watch redesign started
from "what should the watch do" and produced a smaller phone. It only became a
real design when the question changed to "where are you standing when you reach
for this," and nine concrete situations produced the answer in one pass.

So every surface below is specified as: *situations → jobs → what it must not
do*. A feature that cannot name the situation it serves is a feature nobody
asked for.

## The product job

> **Keep a fleet of parallel agents moving, from wherever you happen to be.**

Every surface serves this. They differ only in how much attention the situation
can spare, and that is the single variable that decides everything else.

## The seven jobs

One vocabulary across all surfaces, so two surfaces doing the same job can be
compared rather than merely coexisting.

| # | Job | The user's sentence | Attention |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reassure** | "Is anything wrong?" | A glance. None. |
| 2 | **Unblock** | "One needs an answer." | A glance and a tap. |
| 3 | **Capture** | "I had an idea. Start it before I lose it." | A committed moment. |
| 4 | **Decide** | "This needs judgment. Show me enough." | Minutes, focused. |
| 5 | **Drive** | "I'm working now." | Sustained. Both hands. |
| 6 | **Review** | "Is this good enough to land?" | Sustained, careful. |
| 7 | **Tend** | "Set it up, pair it, configure it." | Rare, deliberate. |

Jobs 1 and 2 are *reactive*: the fleet initiated and you respond. Job 3 is
*yours*. Jobs 4 through 7 are *work*.

### Stolen and committed moments

The distinction that decides which surfaces may have flows:

- **Stolen moment.** The window belongs to something else: a traffic light, a
  meeting, a set at the gym, a stride. The interaction must survive being
  abandoned at any instant with nothing half-sent. One tap, no accumulated
  state.
- **Committed moment.** You stopped on purpose. Full attention, as long as you
  like. Multi-step flows are fine here, and only here.
- **Serial committed moments.** A run of short committed windows with your own
  activity between them: the gym between sets, most obviously. Each window is
  yours and long enough to think in, but it ends on your own schedule and comes
  back 90 seconds later. Multi-step flows are fine **only if they resume where
  they were left**, many times over, without re-establishing context.

Reassure and Unblock must work in stolen moments on every surface. Capture,
Decide, Drive, Review and Tend are committed-moment jobs. Anything aimed at a
serial committed moment must additionally be resumable, because it will be
interrupted by design rather than by accident.

Where you are decides what you reach for. Compiled from the user's own accounts.

situations where you have attention but no desk; the Mac owns the desk.**

## Mac

**Situation.** At the desk. One situation, two modes, and the user confirms both
earn their place.

**Job.** *Console that becomes a cockpit.*

> Show me my whole fleet, let me drop into the one that needs me, and get me
> back out.

The Mac is the only surface that does all seven jobs, and the only one where
Drive, Review and Tend belong at all.

**Two modes:**

| Mode | Job | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Console** | Reassure, Unblock | `SidebarViews`, `FleetStore`, ⌘⌃N Next Needing Attention |
| **Cockpit** | Drive, Review, Decide | `PaneCanvas`, `TerminalPane`, `TileView`, `ChangesPane`, Layout menu |

**The design problem is the transition, not either mode.** The loop is glance →
drop in → act → leave.

Correction (2026-08-21): an earlier draft said the two modes were "bridged by
exactly one command (⌘⌃N)". That was wrong. There are three routes in, and the
best of them is well designed: the sidebar's filter, ⌃⌘N for whatever is
blocked, and **⌘P** — a switcher with live screen previews, recency ordering from
`VisitLog` following Alt-Tab's rule exactly (the pane you are in is not the first
entry), and results that mix navigating with creating. `CommandPalette.swift` and
`VisitLog.swift` are among the most carefully reasoned files in the app.

What remains unexamined is the **leave** step, and whether the Mac needs a
console *view* at all or whether the sidebar plus ⌘P already is one.

**Must not:** become a general terminal emulator, or compete with the user's
editor. `OpenInEditor` exists precisely so it doesn't have to.

## Phone

**Situations.** Couch or bed, in transit, standing with the phone in hand, at
the gym between sets.

**Job.** *Do real work without a desk.*

> When I have attention but no keyboard, let me understand what's happening,
> judge it properly, and act.

The phone is not a glance device that occasionally does more. It is where work
happens when the Mac is not in front of you, and the user is explicit: *"I want
to work properly while I'm at the gym, and that often involves reviewing code."*

**Owns:** Reassure, Unblock, Capture, Decide, **Review**. **Must not:** Tend
beyond pairing, or grow a Layout menu. Tiling panes on a phone serves no
situation on the list.

**Review is a first-class phone job, not a couch luxury.** This is the correction
that reshaped this document. Reviewing a diff is the thing the user most wants to
do at the gym, which means the phone's diff experience is load-bearing rather
than vestigial, and it must work in 60-to-90-second resumable bursts.

**The gym boundary is instructive.** The user named the gym for the phone and
explicitly rejected it for the watch — because the gym is a *work* situation,
and the watch cannot do work. It is a serial committed moment: you control when
the window opens and closes, so the window is long enough to read a diff in, but
a review that cannot be put down and resumed at set 4 is a review that never
finishes. That is a boundary derived from a person rather than from a platform,
which is the only kind that holds.

## Live Activity

**Situation.** Phone in a pocket or face-down on a table. Screen locked. The
user is doing something else entirely.

**Job.** *Follow one run without opening anything.*

> One agent, for the length of its turn, on a locked screen.

The one surface scoped to **a single agent** rather than the fleet. It exists
for the moment an agent stops for an answer while the phone is away, which is
also exactly when Unblock is worth the most.

**Owns:** Reassure (for one run). **Should own but does not:** Unblock. See
finding F1.

**Must not:** try to represent the fleet. That is the widgets' job, and a card
that listed five agents would be a widget with worse ergonomics.

## Phone widgets

**Situation.** Home screen or lock screen, mid-scroll, not looking for anything.

**Job.** *Answer "is anything wrong" before you decide to ask.*

Six families (`.systemSmall/Medium/Large`, `.accessoryInline/Circular/Rectangular`),
all rendering `FleetSnapshot` from the App Group. Fleet-wide, never per-agent.

**Owns:** Reassure, and nothing else. **Must not:** attempt Decide. A widget
that showed transcript prose would be unreadable at that size and stale by
construction.

`FarCoolerNotify` has no job of its own. It is plumbing that keeps this surface
honest while the app is not running, and should be understood as part of the
widgets rather than as a surface.

---

## Watch app

**Situations.** Running, bike at a light, walking, hiking, dinner, meeting,
cooking, swimming, pulled over.

**Job.** *Keep the fleet moving while you're busy doing something else.*

**Owns:** Reassure, Unblock, Capture. Decide is available but demoted, one tap
deep and prefetched, because the only situation with a long reading window
(hiking) is also the one with no network.

**Must not:** pursue parity with the phone. Nothing you do while running or at a
dinner table wants a settings screen.

Full design, including the transport constraint that watchOS forbids sockets
outside an audio session:
`~/.gstack/projects/overnight/e-liang-main-design-20260819-233251.md`.

## Watch complication

**Situation.** A watch face, glanced at for the time.

**Job.** *Reassure without raising the app at all.*

Three families. Renders from cached `FleetSnapshot` with **zero network**, which
is not an optimization but the only way it can work in the situations it serves.

**Owns:** Reassure. **Must not:** anything else. A complication cannot act, and
the honest response to that is to make it excellent at the one thing it can do.

## Cross-surface laws

These already hold in the code. Written down so the next surface inherits them
rather than rediscovering them.

1. **The host decides; surfaces draw.** `glyph`, `headline`, `line`, `rank` and
   `feed` are derived once by the daemon. No client re-derives. This is what
   stops a watch, a widget and a Mac from disagreeing about one pane.
   (`FleetSnapshotWriter.swift`, `FleetListView.swift`.)
2. **Age is part of the value.** Every surface asks `confidence(in:at:)` rather
   than trusting `status`. `blocked` and `done` stay true at any age because only
   a person undoes them; `working` does not.
3. **Never offer an action you cannot deliver.** `WatchState`'s three states, and
   the rule that buttons die with the link.
4. **The agent words its own buttons.** No surface invents an Allow/Deny pair or
   reinterprets a permission's options. The daemon declines to invent that
   vocabulary and so does everything downstream.
5. **Answers are never queued; captures always are.** An answer targets an id
   that can go stale, so late delivery can reverse a decision. A dictated task
   has no id and stays valid.
6. **Amber means "needs you", everywhere.** Reserved across widget, Live
   Activity, complication and app so a glance at any surface answers the
   question without reading a word.

## Audit findings

### F1. Every glance surface is read-only. There is not one `AppIntent` in the tree.

> **Closed for the Live Activity, 2026-08-21.** The leader's card now carries
> the agent's own answers as `Button(intent:)`, on the lock screen and in the
> expanded Island, through `AnswerPermissionIntent`. That grep now returns three
> files. It remains open for the widgets and the complication — see open
> question 1, which the same work narrowed rather than answered.

As of 2026-08-20:
`grep -rn "AppIntent\|Button(intent:" apps/ios --include=*.swift` returned
nothing. The Live Activity fired *precisely* when an agent blocked — that is its
trigger — and offered no way to answer it. Same for every widget and the
complication. Each could only deep-link into the app.

Unblock is the highest-value job in the product, and the surface closest to it
cannot do it. iOS 17+ supports interactive Live Activities and widgets through
`AppIntent`. **This is the largest single gap the audit found**, and it is
probably a smaller change than the watch redesign.

### F2. The phone opens into a cockpit, but three of its four situations are glance situations.

> **Closed 2026-08-21.** `landing = connection.fleet.landingTerminal` is gone
> and `Fleet.landingTerminal` with it. A connected phone opens onto
> `NeedsYouView` — blocked agents and workspaces wanting review — and the drop
> into a terminal is one tap from there. Reassure is the front door; Drive is
> behind it, which is the ordering the situations argued for.

As of 2026-08-20: `FleetView` auto-landed into a terminal on connect (`landing =
connection.fleet.landingTerminal`), and its own doc comment described the fleet
screen as "mostly a hand-off." That is right for the couch. At the gym, in
transit, or standing in a kitchen, the first thing wanted is Reassure, and the
app instead opened a terminal.

### F3. Capture is implemented three times and was named zero times.

`QuickCreate.swift` on the Mac states the insight exactly:

> "The old flow was: open a sheet, pick a repository, type a name, type a
> branch, press Create, wait, find the new worktree, make a terminal, choose an
> agent, wait for it to boot, then finally type what you actually wanted. Nine
> interactions before the first useful word, which is long enough to lose the
> thought that started it."

`QuickTask.swift` ports it to the phone specifically so the two cannot drift.
The watch design then re-derived the same job from scratch last week as "job 3"
— and very nearly cut it, because no document said it was a job. Naming it is
the whole point of this document.

### F4. The gym belongs to the phone, and it is a *work* situation.

User-stated, and counterintuitive twice over. The gym is the canonical watch
situation and it was explicitly rejected for the watch — because what the user
wants to do there is **review code**, and a watch cannot. *"I want to work
properly while I'm at the gym, and that often involves reviewing code."*

Three consequences. The watch needs no gym mode. The phone's Review experience
is load-bearing, not a scaled-down Mac feature. And Review must survive being
interrupted every 90 seconds by design, which is a resumability requirement no
current screen was built against.

### F5. The product design doc contradicts the shipped product.

`farcooler-design.md` constraints say:

> "No background mobile awareness in MVP: push notifications, the APNs relay,
> Live Activities, and Dynamic Island presentations are out of scope entirely.
> Not gated, not stubbed, not behind a capability boundary."

All four now ship. The decision to build them was clearly right, but that
document is the only product-level artifact in the repo and it currently argues
against the code. It is a dated design record and should not be rewritten — but
it needs a dated note, the way its `host` → `runner` vocabulary change already
carries one.

### F6. Three fleet-wide glance surfaces, no stated division.

`FleetWidget` (6 families), `WatchFleetWidget` (3 families) and the Live Activity
all render from `FleetSnapshot` via `SnapshotStore`. The real division is that
**the Live Activity is per-run and the widgets are per-fleet** — which is
correct, and which no document states. They were built in a single plan
(`2026-08-17-glance-surfaces.md`) that specified the mechanism and never the
job, so nothing prevents the next change from blurring them.

## Open questions

1. **Interactive glance surfaces (F1): how far?** ~~A Live Activity with the
   agent's own option buttons is the obvious first move.~~ **Shipped
   2026-08-21.** What remains: does the same apply to the `.systemLarge` widget,
   or does a home screen widget with live buttons invite mis-taps in a pocket?
   And the complication — an accessory widget is narrower than the watch screen
   where the reject button was already found falling below the fold, so the
   honest answer there is probably no.
2. ~~**What is the phone's front door (F2)?**~~ **Resolved 2026-08-21:** land
   on what needs you, and make the drop into a terminal one tap. Auto-landing is
   gone. The question that replaces it: should a *finished* agent appear on that
   screen? `done` means "idle the user has not seen yet" and the daemon already
   treats it as wanting attention, but the front door lists only blocked agents
   and reviewable diffs — so a night of agents that finished having committed
   nothing reads as "Nothing Needs You."
3. ~~Does Review belong on mobile at all?~~ **Resolved 2026-08-20: yes, and it
   is a headline phone job.** The open question that replaces it: what does a
   diff review look like when it must be readable one-handed and resumable
   across a dozen 90-second windows? `ChangesView` was not built for that.
4. **Is Tend a Mac-only job?** Pairing must work on the phone (the watch depends
   on it). Everything else — repositories, editors, themes, runner enrollment —
   may not belong on a phone at all.
5. **Where does Capture live on the phone?** The watch will have dictation. The
   phone has `QuickTask` behind the app. Neither is reachable from a widget or
   the lock screen, which is where an idea actually arrives.
