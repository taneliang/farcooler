Teams

Your whole team works through one orchestrator, each person through their own rep.

Experimental. Teams are experimental, and the CLI labels the plan experimental.

One orchestrator, a rep for each person

A workspace has one lead, who talks to its orchestrator directly. At first the lead is the runner’s owner, who can hand it on. Everyone else talks to their own rep: an agent that carries their asks to the orchestrator and brings its questions back. A rep never answers for anyone, and it can’t start work or change the plan. It can read the plan, the tasks and Needs You, and send messages and comments.

Today the lead makes the decisions; teammates ask through their reps.

Whose login does the work

The orchestrator, and the agents it starts, run on the lead’s login. A teammate’s rep runs on that teammate’s own Claude login once they’ve signed in to Claude on the runner. Until then it runs on the runner’s own login, unless the runner’s admins require members’ own accounts, in which case it can’t start until they sign in. Member Logins covers what a shared login does and doesn’t share.

The team directory

At farcooler.com/app, you sign in, create a team, and make invite links. A link works until a date you choose, for a number of people, and can be limited to one email domain. Admins change roles and remove or readmit members.

Adding a teammate to a runner

On the Mac, scan a teammate’s device with Add Device or Runner…, then Add Teammate. Far Cooler adds that device’s key to the SSH keys of each runner you choose, and records it as theirs.

One runner, one Unix account. Everyone on a shared runner works as the same Unix user, so the rules above keep the team’s shape and stop mistakes, but they aren’t a security boundary. Share a runner only with people you’d trust with that account.
