Teams

How a team shares one runner and one orchestrator: who gets in, who sees what, and who gets told.

Experimental. Teams are experimental, and the CLI labels the plan experimental.

Set up a team in four steps

  1. Make the team. At farcooler.com/app, sign in and create a team. The team is the directory of who belongs.
  2. Invite people. Make an invite link. It works until a date you choose, for a number of people, and can be limited to one email domain. It can add people as members or as billing contacts, never as admins. Admins can change roles and remove or readmit people.
  3. Add each teammate’s device to a runner. On the Mac, scan their device with Add Device or Runner…, choose Read or Control, then click Add Teammate. Far Cooler adds that device’s key to the SSH keys of each runner you choose, one runner at a time, and records the device as theirs. A teammate gets no shell access this way.
  4. Decide whose login does the work. See Logins below.

A teammate added by hand, without a Far Cooler account, works the same way but gets no push notifications.

Members and member admins

A member is a teammate whose devices are recorded as theirs on a runner. Everything they write on the board carries their name, not yours.

A member admin is a member whose device is enrolled with admin access to the runner. Admins can add and remove teammates, change their devices, and require members’ own accounts in Member Logins. You, as the runner’s owner, stay in charge of everything else: see What stays yours.

The Mac’s Add Teammate makes ordinary members only: it offers Read and Control, never admin. To make a member admin, run these on the runner, as the owner:

  1. farcooler client enroll --key "<the device's public key>" --label "<device name>" --client-id <device id> --scope host_admin
  2. farcooler member add <handle> --name "<Name>" --device <device id>

The first command lets the device administer the runner. The scope has no default on purpose, so a forgotten flag can’t grant it. The second binds that device to the member; until then it counts as yours. Use the same device id in both.

A member admin can enroll a teammate’s device from their own Mac, the same way you do. A device they enroll can only read until they bind it to a teammate with Add Teammate. It never becomes yours, and it can’t be given shell access. A member admin also can’t rebind or revoke your devices. If a member admin’s enrollment is interrupted before the binding, the device stays read-only. To find such a device, compare farcooler client list with farcooler member list.

One orchestrator, a rep for each person

A workspace has one lead, who talks to its orchestrator directly. At first the lead is the runner’s owner, who can hand it on to a member. Only the owner or the lead can start or replace the workspace’s orchestrator.

Everyone else talks to their own rep: an agent that carries their asks to the orchestrator and brings its questions back. A rep never answers for anyone, and it can’t start work. It can read the plan, the tasks and Needs You, send messages and comments, and change the plan when its member asks. A change a rep makes carries its member’s name. Only its member, or you, can answer a rep’s questions.

The plan belongs to the whole team. Anyone can change it, from their own devices or through their rep, and the orchestrator works from what it says.

Every member can read a workspace’s charter, the rules its work is done by. On a Mac that isn’t the runner, Show Charter opens it in a sheet you can read but not change. The themes’ order is the team’s one ranking, so any member, or their rep, can move a theme with farcooler plan theme move <name> --to <n>. A device whose teammate was removed can’t read it, or anything else.

Roles

Everyone you add starts as a Member. To trust a teammate with the workspaces’ rules, make them a Maintainer: in the runner’s settings, People shows you a role beside each teammate, or run farcooler member role <handle> maintainer. Only you can give a role.

  • A Maintainer can edit any workspace’s charter, rename a workspace, change its task prefix, and set its pull request size, cost line, nudge hours and Wake the Agent When You Answer.
  • How a workspace lands, onto which branch, turning the nudge on and who leads it stay yours: each of those acts with your credentials.
  • A workspace’s lead can change that workspace’s charter, and so can you. On a Mac that isn’t the runner, Show Charter has Edit for the three of you, and reads only for everyone else.

farcooler member log lists who changed whose role, who was added or removed, who changed a charter, who renamed a workspace or changed its prefix, and who handed on its lead. Removing a teammate also takes their devices’ access away. Each device is told it no longer has access, and its view of the runner’s work is cleared: at once if Far Cooler is open on it when you do, or, for one that connects later at the runner’s address, after a few seconds of refusals. One that reaches the runner only through Tailcat and isn’t open at the time can only be told the runner isn’t answering, because Tailcat says nothing to a device it doesn’t know.

Logins

The orchestrator, and the agents it starts, run on the lead’s login. A teammate’s own panes and their rep run either on the runner’s shared login or on the teammate’s own. By default a teammate may use the runner’s login, which spends your account and quota, and a teammate who has signed in to Claude on the runner keeps using their own. Turn on Require Own Claude Account and a teammate’s Claude panes can’t open until they sign in.

Member Logins covers the switches for Claude, Codex and Cursor, and what a shared login does and doesn’t share.

Who gets told: Needs You

Needs You is the list of everything waiting on a person. Each item belongs to the person it concerns, so each teammate can tell what’s theirs.

  • Yours holds what’s waiting on you: an agent you started, a lane of yours that needs a decision or review, and anything waiting on your rep. It also holds items that belong to nobody, such as a task waiting with no agent on it.
  • Everyone Else holds the rest, each row naming whose it is: a member’s name, or Owner for the runner’s owner. When nothing belongs to anyone else, the list is one flat list, as it is for a solo owner.

On a runner with a team, every row says whose it is, in one word or name: You, a member’s name, Owner, or Anyone for an item that belongs to nobody. Under the Yours heading a row doesn’t repeat “You.” A runner with no members says nothing, because there is nobody to tell apart. This is the same on the Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android.

Routing decides the order, the grouping and the pushes. It doesn’t hide anything: every member sees every item. Any member can answer any item from an ordinary lane. The exceptions are an orchestrator’s questions, which go to its lead and you, and another member’s rep.

Answering someone else’s item asks first. Before an answer goes to an item that is another person’s, Far Cooler asks “This is for Bob. Answer for them?” and sends it only if you choose Answer. This covers an agent’s permission request, an agent’s question and a decision on a task, on the Mac, iPhone, iPad, Android and Apple Watch (where the alert names a first name so Answer stays on screen). Your own items and items that belong to nobody send straight away. A decision answered for someone shows on the task’s note as “Alice for Bob.”

Buttons that can’t ask don’t appear. A button on a lock screen or in a notification can’t show that question, so it doesn’t offer the answer. The agent’s Live Activity shows no buttons for a teammate’s ask; it says “This is for Bob. Tap the card to answer,” and you answer in the app. A notification button for someone else’s decision is refused the same way, and a Mac banner for a teammate’s decision has no answer buttons. Opening it takes you to the task, where the question is asked.

An answered ask says who answered. On a runner with members, an ask answered from a device reads “You answered,” “Bob answered,” “Alice answered for Bob” or “Alice answered for you” in the conversation, on the Mac, iPhone and Android. The runner names the person from the device that sent the answer, never from what anyone typed. An ask answered at the keyboard names no one.

Pushes. When an item belongs to a member with a Far Cooler account, that member’s phones get the push, and you aren’t buzzed for it. Your card still moves, without a sound. You still get the push for items that are yours or nobody’s, for pull request review requests, and for a member’s item when no push reached them: a member added by hand, a member whose devices have that kind of push off, or one whose push couldn’t be sent. A member gets at most one of these pushes every two minutes per runner. Member admins aren’t pushed about items that belong to nobody; you are. Tapping a push opens its item on the iPhone and Android. Members are pushed only about their own items; they see everyone’s in Needs You.

Whose pane is it?

Some marks and answers have to be filed under a person even when the call comes from a terminal on the runner instead of a phone. A call from a device counts as that device’s teammate. A call from a pane counts as the person whose login the pane runs on. A rep’s pane is its member, and so is any other pane on a member’s login: their shell, their agents, an orchestrator they lead. So Bob typing farcooler terminal agent-answer in his own shell is Bob. A pane on a login whose member was removed is nobody’s and marks nothing. A pane on the runner’s own login, and the Mac app, count as you. It follows the login, not the keyboard, so you typing in Bob’s pane from the Mac app counts as Bob there.

What a member can see and do

  • Members read everything you do. That includes every workspace’s plan, its themes and your asks on them, the tasks, Needs You and the runner’s settings.
  • They can change the plan. Their devices can change the order of lanes, a page, a theme, a lane, a train or a ruling, and keep or reverse a ruling. Integration and its record stay with you and the orchestrator: moving a lane to landing or landed, or moving a lane that’s already there, dropping it included; putting a lane on a train or taking it off; starting a train, or changing its lanes, base or state; a lane’s or a train’s commit; a lane’s branch or checkout; a lane’s agent; and marking a reversal done with its commit. A member can rename a train. The rulings list reads Open Decisions for them.
  • They can do the day-to-day work. They can create tasks and workspaces, add notes, change a task’s status, open and use lanes and terminals, and answer agents’ questions.
  • Other people’s lanes are answered, not driven. On your lanes and other teammates’, a member can answer an agent’s question, after confirming, and press Stop. They can’t type into the pane, restart it or remove it.
  • Reading marks are each person’s own. What you have read on a board, which worktrees you have reviewed, whether a finished pane still says Done, and whether you are watching a pane are kept per person. A member opening a card or a pane clears nothing for you, your look clears nothing for them, and a member watching a pane doesn’t silence your push. A person’s marks follow them across their own devices. The review count that the relay shows on the lock screen and widgets is still yours alone.
  • Their writes carry their name. A card, note, message or plan change from a member shows that member’s name on every client. You get a notification for a member’s card, note or message, and their own devices don’t. A plan change, a kept or reversed ruling included, sends no notification: the plan updates on every open client, and the change is in the plan’s history under their name. Nobody can write as you except you.

What stays yours

These are refused to every member’s device, admin or not:

  • Acting on GitHub as you: Mark Ready and replying to a pull request thread. Turning the PR nudge on is yours too; a member can turn it off.
  • Renaming, re-prefixing or deleting a workspace, and changing a workspace’s other settings.
  • Changing the runner: repository roots, the branch prefix, the projector, registering a repository, removing a worktree and its files, and stopping the daemon.
  • Turning off a Member Logins switch. Admins can require own accounts, not relax it.
  • Starting or replacing an orchestrator they don’t lead, or handing off a lead.
  • Starting another teammate’s rep. Each member starts their own.

A few things a member can still reach with your login, and these are the boundary of what Far Cooler can enforce. A member’s shell pane runs as the runner’s Unix user, with that user’s gh and git. Far Cooler refuses a member typing into your lane’s pane, but their shell pane can still reach it as the runner’s Unix user. A member’s read of a pull request’s status uses your gh login, but only to read.

Removing a teammate from a runner

farcooler member remove <handle> takes their devices’ keys off the runner, stops their rep, and deletes their Claude login there. It also revokes every device they enrolled that was never bound to anyone. A member admin can do this too. If one of their devices is still connected, it reads nothing: not the board, the plan, the charter or Needs You. It can’t change anything, and it gets no notifications.

Taking a device off a teammate removes its key from the runner and disconnects it at once, the same as removing the teammate does. To move a device to another teammate, add it again with Add Teammate. A device taken off a teammate never becomes yours, even if it’s your own phone, so only list a device under a teammate if it’s theirs.

What they wrote stays on the board, under their handle. Their name is looked up by that handle, so if you later add someone else with the same handle, their old notes show the new person’s name. Give a different person a different handle.

One runner, one Unix account. Everyone on a shared runner works as the same Unix user, so the rules above keep the team’s shape and stop mistakes, but they aren’t a security boundary. Share a runner only with people you’d trust with that account.
