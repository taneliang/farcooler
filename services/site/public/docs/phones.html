Phones and the Watch

Answer what needs you from anywhere, and read how the work is going.

Not available yet. The iPhone, Apple Watch and Android apps aren’t publicly distributed. Until they are, they’re built from the source.

iPhone and Android

The phone apps have the same shape as the Mac: Needs You first, answerable in place, then each workspace with its orchestrator, its plan and board, and its worktrees and terminals. Notifications tell you when an agent or the orchestrator needs you, and tapping one opens what it’s about.

Screens shown are from Android; the iPhone app has the same ones.

They need iOS 26 or Android 17 or later.

Appearance on Android

In Settings, Appearance sets whether the app is light or dark: Theme, System, Light or Dark. System follows the phone and is the default. Theme follows the terminal theme you chose, as the app did before this setting existed, so choose it to keep that look. Anyone who hasn’t chosen yet now gets System. The terminal always keeps its own theme.

Apple Watch

The watch shows what needs you. You can allow or deny an agent’s request right there; decisions and reviews say Open on iPhone. On a team runner each row says whose it is, and allowing or denying a teammate’s request asks “Answer for them?” first. It needs watchOS 26 or later.

How a phone reaches your runner

A phone talks to a runner over SSH, with a key the phone makes and keeps. You add a phone by scanning a code on the Mac, with both signed in to the same account, and the runner adds the phone’s key to its SSH keys. See What Runs Where for the details.
