The `iOS` job did acquire that label at 14:15:13, and `iOS UI (smoke)` acquired it at 14:21:24. This establishes that the label was serving jobs during the same window. The job records expose assignment and cancellation, and GitHub's annotation gives no scheduler-level explanation for why the Swift requests went unserved. The engine used the attempt conclusion as the landing verdict. Attempt 1 reported `failure` even though its job list contained no job with conclusion `failure`. The failed-log lookup therefore had no job to name, and the completed attempt could settle landing group 6161 and ov-1406 while the automatic retry was running. A `cancelled` conclusion could also settle the wait through its cancellation route.

## Retry behavior

- The retry workflow still handles main-push CI. A runnerless cancellation qualifies when GitHub's check annotation confirms acquisition failure. A copied cancellation can recover its annotation from a runnerless cancellation with the same job name and completion timestamp. The retry uses the current job ID and prioritizes older unserved jobs when more than one qualifies.
- Automatic acquisition retries stop after **eight CI attempts total**, including the original and any manual attempts. Each completed attempt can reserve one automatic request. The existing first-attempt retry for actual test failures shares that reservation. After the ceiling, a cancelled required job leaves CI non-green.
- GitHub reports each attempt as completed, which triggers the retry workflow. The board and the engine follow the retry workflow for the exact run ID and attempt. They give a missing completion event up to 15 minutes to appear and keep waiting while a matching retry workflow is queued or running. After it completes, they check the source run again for a newer attempt. The landing waits through those controller and rerun states even after its original CI deadline elapses. The deadline still applies to an ordinary pending CI run. If a terminal attempt still has a required job cancelled before runner assignment, the landing records the job name and the acquisition failure when those job records are available.
- The Python regression replays the original and copied check records from this incident. Against the old selector it fails because attempt 2 produces no retry selection. The daemon regression advances the landing clock past the original deadline while the retry controller is active. It follows the failure, cancellation and running rerun through to either success or a terminal cancellation or failure with a recorded reason.
