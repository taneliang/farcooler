# ov-1098: Mac performance measurements and fix

## ov-1151 rebase note

Upstream ov-1062 now supplies origin, duration, and agent-type words on the
rows, removing the per-item bridge calls and their cache. The rebase retains
phrase-table preparation in the ledger and its cold-process readiness gate.
The measurements and test counts below describe the earlier implementation,
not this rebased version; they do not establish that the CI gates pass here.

On Nov 14 the rebased checkout passed the cold readiness test, all 2,113
AgentKit tests, and the two row-budget tests. Local Mac performance tests
passed: first-page maximum 50 ms, streaming maximum 17 ms, and median fling
hitch 6.2 ms/s. These ran without the machine-wide lock while another job
held it; they are not controlled CI-runner measurements.

The fix prepares conversation words in the off-main-actor ledger before rows
reach the view, including the first phrase-table fetch. It keeps formatting
in Rust and preserves the CI limits: 100 ms for first-page/streaming stalls
and 75 ms/s for fling hitches. The final local performance run measured
27 ms for first page, 19.3 ms for streaming and 5.2 ms/s for fling. These
are local verification, not a new CI run of this change.

## Initial investigation and origin-cache experiment (Nov 12)

The Nov 11 rerun is the same commit, not a subsequent fix. The Nov 6 and 7
fling measurements also use the same commit: the runner varies substantially.
The first-page test, however, rose from 72–94 ms to 107–125 ms in these
samples. This is too little evidence to separate a regression from a change
in runner load, and the rerun still exceeded the 100 ms first-page budget.
The first-page increase was already visible by Nov 9, before the conversation
words bridge changes in the Nov 10 commit; those changes alone cannot explain
the entire trend. The transcript's ordering changes landed between the Nov 8
and Nov 9 commits and deserve a controlled comparison, but the two runs do not
isolate them from runner variation.

The first-page budget remains 100 ms as requested in ov-1102. Neither an
increase to the fling budget nor a performance fix is justified by these
samples alone. Repeat the gates on both sides of the suspected changes on the
same runner, alternating builds and recording CPU time and first-page stalls,
before choosing a fix or a fling threshold.

The Nov 11 job logs also report 12 of 50 items built in both attempts. In
that same test process the streaming test also opens the first page:
its ungated first-page maximum was 75 ms in attempt 1 and 39 ms in attempt
2. The gated test measured 125 and 107 ms respectively. This comparison
does not identify which work accounts for the difference;
the two openings also occur at different times and with different sources.
Fling main-thread CPU per tick was 5.84 and 5.79 ms in those attempts while
hitch medians were 81.3 and 63.2 ms/s. The hitch variation alone therefore
does not establish a change in application CPU cost.

On Nov 12, a local run of the first-page test built 12 of 50 items and
measured 50.6 ms against the 50 ms local limit (a failure). The preceding
empty-window measurement included a 400 ms maximum; this single run on a
busy machine cannot establish a before/after difference.

The Nov 2–5 logs extend the baseline. Nov 4–7 used the same commit;
its fling medians ranged from 3.1 to 57.0 ms/s, and the three recorded
first-page maxima were 73, 72, and 89 ms. The Nov 5 log has a fling
measurement but no first-page output, so it is not counted as a first-page
sample. These older runs reinforce the runner-variation concern but do not
resolve the first-page increase or justify a threshold change.

On Nov 12, the Nov 10 conversation-words change was found to call the
core through JSON for every turn's origin, including repeated identical
origins while estimating and drawing the first page. The origin answers
are now cached, but failures are not cached, so a later bridge response can
still correct the display. The cache is bounded because origin is wire data.

On this Mac, six separate first-page runs with the cache measured 46, 46,
34, 34, 35 and 49 ms; six without it measured 35, 28, 56, 52, 38 and
38 ms (another initial uncached run measured 53 ms). These are sequential,
not alternating, and overlap substantially. They do not establish that
the cache fixes the CI regression or that the 100 ms runner gate holds.
The Nov 9 increase also predates the bridge change. A controlled comparison
on the CI runner and investigation of the earlier increase remain needed.

The two Nov 12 samples were read on Nov 13. They are manual runs on
`ov-1096-ipad-chat-turn` and `ov-1094-plan-sheet-page-2`, not main, so they
are labeled separately. The second run's green
Mac job does not mean the nightly workflow passed, or that ov-1098's change
was tested on CI. Sources: [the first branch's Mac job](https://github.com/taneliang/farcooler/actions/runs/43286220413/job/131421792411)

Their streaming maxima were 282 and 26 ms respectively. On Nov 8,
the 152.4 ms/s fling cost 6.23 ms of main-thread CPU per tick, versus 5.84
and 5.79 in Nov 11's attempts and 4.27 and 4.75 in the new samples. These
are different commits and runners, not a controlled comparison.

## Nov 13: move conversation words out of the first layout

The conversation phrase-table change (`f864f4091`, ov-1049) and item-logic
change (`e6b8f7fc7`, ov-1054) reached the Nov 10 CI build. They replaced native
words and scalar logic with JSON requests in row bodies and height estimation.
The origin-only cache on this branch left duration requests in every render,
and still made the first origin request during layout. An instrumented local
first page made 14 item requests, spending 22.5 ms inside those calls, and
failed at 74 ms. It evaluated only 7 of 25 individual row bodies and placed
12 of 50 items: eager offscreen body evaluation was ruled out.

Caching the remaining item answers alone was insufficient. Its first local
page passed at 39 ms, but three alternating origin-cache/item-cache pairs
measured 35/80, 33/46 and 39/43 ms. They do not show a consistent improvement;
one item-cache run failed the unchanged 50 ms local limit. Background load
also rose during that experiment. Those numbers are retained rather than
counted as evidence that memoization alone fixes the gate.

Preparing only scalar answers also left a cold bridge call: the phrase
table fetched by the first turn footer and tool-group title. Two alternating
unprepared/scalar-prepared pairs measured 50/36 and 62/65 ms; scalar
preparation alone still failed once. The cold-process regression check then
confirmed that the phrase table was absent when the ledger returned rows.

The final fix prepares the phrase table and the changed rows' origin,
duration and agent-type answers in `AgentRowLedger.stamped`, before handing
the delta to the main actor.
The ledger already decodes and groups rows off the main actor. It now does
the first item bridge calls there too; views reuse the core's answers from
a locked, 256-entry FIFO cache. A follow prepares only changed rows, never
walks the held window on each streaming update. Failed or malformed answers
are not cached, so views can retry them. Formatting remains in Rust, and
all performance limits remain unchanged.

The regression check failed with seven missing cached answers before the
ledger call was added, then passed with it: turn origins and durations,
tool/thinking/subagent durations, agent types, and a changed duration in a
follow are ready before the ledger returns. The added phrase-table assertion
also failed before that table was prepared, then passed with it. Separately,
bypassing memo hits made the repeated-duration test call the bridge 50 times
rather than once.
Retry, field separation (including `false`), eviction and public-helper
wiring are covered too. The full AgentKit run passed all 2,116 tests with
the leak check and completion floor. The short, untimed regression runs
used a higher light-run load allowance; they are not performance samples.

## Complete-fix verification (Nov 13)

Builds and measurements were separate, under the shared-machine heavy lock.
Each timed invocation waited for the five-minute load average below 20.
The comparison toggled only `ConversationWords.prepare(delta.rows)`; the
same item cache was present on both sides. Every measurement was a fresh
test process, alternating preparation off/on on the same Mac.
The existing first-page measurement still starts at `store.start` and
includes preparation; the timing harness and its assertions are unchanged.

| Pair | Preparation off | Complete preparation on |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 65 ms (failed) | 39 ms |
| 2 | 34 ms | 31 ms |

The later complete streaming-suite invocation measured a 27 ms first page,
with 12 of 50 items built and the reader at the tail, and a 19.3 ms maximum
streaming stall. All three complete-preparation first-page samples passed
the unchanged 50 ms local limit. The small, overlapping sample cannot
establish a CI distribution; the deterministic readiness regression proves
the covered phrase-table and row-item lookups are ready before layout.

Fling measured 5.2 ms/s with 1.99 ms of main-thread CPU per tick, passing the
40 ms/s local limit. Before this fix, the same Mac measured 15.2 ms/s and
2.01 ms/tick on the rebased origin-cache-only branch. The hitch difference
does not establish a CPU speedup. The much wider same-commit CI hitch spread
above is evidence of runner variation; the new work in first-page layout
is independently identified and removed. The fling budget stays 75 on CI.

A preliminary scalar-only-preparation scrolling run passed its fling gate
at 6.3 ms/s but failed its companion no-row-jump check (296 pt at -12,000
pt/s). The final complete-preparation scrolling run passed both tests;
that single transient failure is not claimed as a separately fixed bug.

The final full AgentKit run passed 2,116 tests in 231 suites, including all
six new cache/readiness tests, with the updated floor and no leaked files.
The final row-budget, streaming and scrolling invocations each passed two
tests. Transcript jumps passed 10 tests, scroller/resize checks passed 31,
and native-agent/polish checks passed 13. Each invocation used completion
guards and leak checks. Shared-core lint, file-size budget, test-leak lint
and `git diff --check` also passed.

## Nov 14: adoption and CI regression coverage

The adopted branch already contains the complete off-main-actor preparation
fix. Its base includes `5b7a40d4e1a8d5647aba6522b57a70c03ce50904`, the run's
`FARCOOLER_BASE`. The measurement table above now links each run attempt,
including the two branch runs labeled separately from nightly main.

The readiness regression now also covers restoring a cached pane and
prepending an older page. Both must prepare unique origins and durations
before returning rows to layout. The nightly Mac perf job runs all seven
conversation-words cache/readiness tests in a separate process after building
the Rust client library, before the timing gates. This makes a missing cold
phrase-table or item preparation fail deterministically even on a fast runner.
The 100 ms first-page/streaming and 75 ms/s CI fling budgets are unchanged.

Fresh verification on Nov 14 bypassed only the ledger's preparation call:
the new restore/older-page test failed at all four readiness assertions
(both origins and both durations). After restoring the call, a fresh process
passed all seven cache/readiness tests, and the full AgentKit suite passed
2,117 tests in 231 suites. Both positive runs satisfied their completion
floors and left no test files or preferences behind. The restore-origin
assertion runs before requesting the older page, so paging cannot hide a
missing restore preparation.

The Rust client library was rebuilt from this checkout with two workers.
The shared heavy-job lock was occupied by another validation job; these
untimed checks used the wrapper's explicit `--no-lock` option. No new timing
samples are claimed here: the production fix is unchanged from the Nov 13
measurements above. Shared-core lint, file-size budget, test-leak lint,
doc-comment check, workflow actionlint (with the existing custom `xcode-27`
runner label configured), and `git diff --check` passed.
