The Nov 11 rerun is the same commit, not a subsequent fix. The Nov 6 and 7
fling measurements also use the same commit: the runner varies substantially.
The first-page test, however, rose from 72–94 ms to 107–125 ms in these
samples. This is too little evidence to separate a regression from a change
in runner load, and the rerun still exceeded the 100 ms first-page budget.
The first-page increase was already visible by Nov 9, before the conversation
words bridge changes in the Nov 10 commit; those changes alone cannot explain
the entire trend. The transcript's ordering changes landed between the Nov 8
and Nov 9 commits and deserve a controlled comparison, but the two runs do not
isolate them from runner variation.

The first-page budget remains 100 ms as requested in ov-1102. Neither an
increase to the fling budget nor a performance fix is justified by these
samples alone. Repeat the gates on both sides of the suspected changes on the
same runner, alternating builds and recording CPU time and first-page stalls,
before choosing a fix or a fling threshold.

The Nov 11 job logs also report 12 of 50 items built in both attempts. In
that same test process the streaming test also opens the first page:
its ungated first-page maximum was 75 ms in attempt 1 and 39 ms in attempt
2. The gated test measured 125 and 107 ms respectively. This comparison
does not identify which work accounts for the difference;
the two openings also occur at different times and with different sources.
Fling main-thread CPU per tick was 5.84 and 5.79 ms in those attempts while
hitch medians were 81.3 and 63.2 ms/s. The hitch variation alone therefore
does not establish a change in application CPU cost.

On Nov 12, a local run of the first-page test built 12 of 50 items and
measured 50.6 ms against the 50 ms local limit (a failure). The preceding
empty-window measurement included a 400 ms maximum; this single run on a
busy machine cannot establish a before/after difference.

The Nov 2–5 logs extend the baseline. Nov 4–7 used the same commit;
its fling medians ranged from 3.1 to 57.0 ms/s, and the three recorded
first-page maxima were 73, 72, and 89 ms. The Nov 5 log has a fling
measurement but no first-page output, so it is not counted as a first-page
sample. These older runs reinforce the runner-variation concern but do not
resolve the first-page increase or justify a threshold change.

On Nov 12, the Nov 10 conversation-words change was found to call the
core through JSON for every turn's origin, including repeated identical
origins while estimating and drawing the first page. The origin answers
are now cached, but failures are not cached, so a later bridge response can
still correct the display. The cache is bounded because origin is wire data.

On this Mac, six separate first-page runs with the cache measured 46, 46,
34, 34, 35 and 49 ms; six without it measured 35, 28, 56, 52, 38 and
38 ms (another initial uncached run measured 53 ms). These are sequential,
not alternating, and overlap substantially. They do not establish that
the cache fixes the CI regression or that the 100 ms runner gate holds.
The Nov 9 increase also predates the bridge change. A controlled comparison
on the CI runner and investigation of the earlier increase remain needed.
