# macOS CI queue investigation (ov-1134)

**Finding:** contention for the five macOS slots, amplified by full, manually dispatched branch Nightlies, explains the observed long waits. This is not evidence that GitHub's runner limit changed. At 06:40:12 UTC on Nov 13, [main CI 43380252837](https://github.com/taneliang/farcooler/actions/runs/43380252837) created its Apple jobs while four shards of [branch Nightly 43374015223](https://github.com/taneliang/farcooler/actions/runs/43374015223) and one Canary macOS job occupied all five slots. CI's iOS job started 66.3 minutes later, Swift tests after 57.2, and the app build after 58.7. The Nightly's phone3 shard itself waited 90.6 minutes. On Nov 12 at 19:27 UTC, [CI 43290333628](https://github.com/taneliang/farcooler/actions/runs/43290333628) arrived while five jobs from [branch Nightly 43286220413](https://github.com/taneliang/farcooler/actions/runs/43286220413) ran; its iOS and Swift test jobs waited 70.3 minutes.

Nightly builds iOS, then releases a fan-out of UI and capture shards alongside a Mac performance job. The checked-in matrix has 12 UI/capture jobs; branch versions in the sample sometimes had more. These long jobs contend with CI's up to four initial macOS jobs and subsequent smoke job, and Canary's two shipping jobs. Nightly's single `concurrency: nightly` serializes Nightly runs with each other, **not** with CI or Canary; its `cancel-in-progress: false` also permits a pending run, not an unlimited FIFO backlog. CI cancels superseded runs per ref. Long waits can therefore occur even when Actions is healthy. The measurements establish contention in these incidents, not a universal cause for every queue or an independently verified account-wide limit.

## Measurements

Window: jobs created from **2026-11-10 07:57:55 UTC** through **2026-11-13 07:57:55 UTC**, from `gh api repos/taneliang/farcooler/actions/runs?created=>=2026-11-10&per_page=100` and each relevant run's `/jobs?per_page=100&filter=all`, paginated. These are job-level ready-to-run (`created_at`) to runner start (`started_at`) delays, not time spent waiting for workflow concurrency or `needs`; duration is start to completion, including setup and teardown. Peak is the maximum overlapping started-to-completed intervals for that workflow, not a sum of the individual peaks. Times are minutes; p90 is the nearest upper observed rank. Skipped jobs and jobs without a runner are excluded. Three still-running CI jobs contribute to queue statistics but not completed duration or peak; totals are lower bounds. The API returned 37 rerun records whose `started_at` predates their new `created_at` and repeats an earlier attempt's interval; these were excluded from all metrics to avoid negative queues and double-counting. Runs created before Nov 10 are outside the query even if rerun during the window. Values reflect the API at collection time, not a live dashboard.

Across these workflows the observed peak was **5**, not the sum of their peaks. No Release run appeared in the queried window. Nightly's manual dispatches used 2,482 completed runner-minutes across 103 jobs, versus 933 across 29 scheduled jobs; manual runs reached the same peak of five. They are the largest measured discretionary source of contention, although some were cancelled or failed and the sample does not establish how many dispatches were necessary. CI's macOS Rust jobs alone used 747 minutes across 19 completed jobs (median 41.5, p90 54.1); Swift tests used 543 across 21 (median 25.7); app builds 299 across 16 (median 19.4); iOS builds 121 across 17 (median 6.8); smoke 103 across 10 (median 10.1). These counts exclude invalid duplicate rerun records and cannot be treated as a complete billing total.

## Recommended changes, ranked by potential runner-minutes saved

Estimates are measured upper bounds or per-run opportunities, not additive forecasts. Retain the owner's laptop exclusion. Check required-check rules and run proof builds before implementing any coverage change.

1. **Replace routine branch full-Nightly dispatches with targeted proof.** Add a `workflow_dispatch` choice in `.github/workflows/nightly.yml` for a targeted UI shard or capture set; condition the matrices and perf job on that choice, keeping scheduled main full and allowing an explicit full branch run. Make targeted proof the branch default, update shard-budget/report logic and its tests for partial runs, and document the full-run escape hatch. The observed manual Nightlies consumed **2,482 min** in three days, the ceiling if all were eliminated, not a prediction: retained targeted jobs, necessary full proofs, and failed/cancelled jobs reduce savings. This also directly reduces main contention. Avoid relying on `max-parallel` as a minute saving: it limits instantaneous pressure but lengthens Nightly wall time and may still compete with main.
2. **Narrow macOS Rust work only after identifying separable coverage.** In `ci.yml`, split the Rust matrix into independent Linux full-suite and macOS platform-specific jobs; move demonstrably platform-independent checks to Linux and retain macOS-specific daemon, filesystem, terminal and integration coverage on macOS. The measured ceiling is **747 min** in the window, or 41.5 min per median completed job; actual savings are the time of checks moved, less setup/duplication. Measure nextest and build steps first, establish a maintained test selection and keep periodic full macOS coverage. Dropping the entire macOS job would lose important platform coverage.
3. **Expand existing path gating only for proven independent inputs.** `scripts/ci-changes.py` already skips app families for site/docs and narrows several platform-specific paths, comparing against the nearest green main ancestor and failing open. Audit the remaining shared scripts and paths against actual test/build reads; add only demonstrably independent patterns to `NARROW`, with positive, negative, mixed-diff and unknown-base tests. A skipped Apple family has a measured median opportunity of roughly **52 min** (19.4 app + 25.7 tests + 6.8 iOS, plus 10.1 smoke when it would run); a skipped Rust Mac job adds 41.5 min where applicable. No reliable frequency or total saving can be inferred without classifying the actual diffs, and these opportunities overlap items 1 and 2.
4. **Profile cache misses and duplicated setup before merging jobs.** CI already uses `Swatinem/rust-cache` and SwiftPM download caches; inspect step timing and cache hit logs for the Rust and Swift jobs, then fix keys or cache only reusable outputs with toolchain/input keys. Savings are unquantified until hit/miss timings are measured. Merging Swift jobs may save duplicated setup but lengthens the critical path and does not remove their test work; measure before choosing it. Moving full UI shards off main CI is already done: only the smoke shard remains on push, so moving the full suite to Nightly again saves nothing. Removing smoke would save at most its measured **103 min** here but loses per-push UI coverage; do not do so without an explicit coverage decision.

For any change, compare the same per-workflow queue p90, completed runner-minutes, peak occupancy and main CI wall time over a comparable three-day period, and verify scheduled full UI coverage and required checks remain effective.
