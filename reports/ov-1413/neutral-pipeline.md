# ov-1413 · Decide the unwired neutral pipeline

The schema-3 pipeline was the planned replacement for the running workflow engine. The pure reducer, coding rules, and state converter landed deliberately unwired. Work reached §10 step 2 of the design, with the planned persistence work still outstanding. The atomic runtime switch in step 3 has no matching implementation or board ticket in the records searched. The board records why individual tickets waited and how they landed. It contains no decision cancelling or deferring the redesign as a whole. The evidence supports **an unfinished planned replacement**, with the reason the runtime ticket was never filed or taken up unrecorded.

## Plan, tickets, and commits

The Nov 15 owner answer on ov-1223 states the product intent: “Having 1 workflow per workspace is probably best for now to limit complexity.” It explicitly includes “non engineering work like being a personal assistant” and says “Def don't pin tickets to workflow versions because that makes it hard to understand the pipeline.” The design turns those decisions into a workspace-wide layout with station queues, admission, and interventions. Coding is the first adapter. Section 10 says, “The new reducer lands unwired; one cutover step switches the runtime, migrates every workspace in one transaction, and deletes the old reducer.” [Design summary and model](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#summary-one-page), [§10 cutover](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#10-cutover).

## Where work stopped and why

- **The early design reviews stopped two design runs.** The ov-1223 manager decision (Nov 15) says “This ticket's own engine run was cancelled as superseded; the work is on main.” ov-1251 reached Needs You after the Nov 15 finding, “Define how a running outward operation completes after its station is replaced.” ov-1253 explicitly continued that design to close the outstanding safety gap. Its Nov 15 manager decision says “This ticket's own engine run is superseded; the work is on main.” The cancellations were of individual runs while the design advanced through carriers.
- **The three implementation tickets were intentionally scoped to stop before service integration.** ov-1266 says “The daemon must not use this module yet”. ov-1273 says “Still unwired: nothing in crates/daemon, crates/cli or crates/store changes and the old reducer is untouched.” ov-1281 repeats “Still unwired: nothing in crates/daemon, crates/cli or crates/store changes.” The three cards are Done. The pure migration ticket did not take on the snapshot table also listed in design step 2.
- **ov-1281 had temporary landing holds.** Its Nov 16 manager note says “Review passed; **held, not queued**” because the branch replayed ov-1273 commits and conflicted when folded. A later note gave ov-1269 priority because “landing speed ranks first, so it goes ahead.” The next note corrected what happened: “Its group had already left for its gate, so it lands first and ov-1269 rebases after it.” The manager subsequently recorded “On main and confirmed: CI is green at `d2e88a5a1`”. ov-1269 landed and updated both test paths. The landing-speed decision explains that conflict and the proposed queue order. It gives no reason for stopping the wider redesign.
- **The next documented action was §10 step 3.** The next board item I found that explicitly asks for a decision about this implementation is ov-1413, prompted by ov-1395's audit. I searched the local Overnight board with `farcooler task search 'cutover' --repo overnight` and used read-only queries against the runner board's ticket titles, intents, acceptance, full notes, rulings and pages for the design path, schema-3 variants, neutral pipeline, `AdapterExec`, redesign, and ov-1281. No matching step-3 ticket, later decision against schema 3, or explanation for the absence surfaced. This establishes a gap in the available record. It cannot distinguish an unfiled handoff from a deliberate choice made elsewhere. ## Work the design still requires

1. **Persist and rehearse the migration.** The design calls for an immutable schema-3 snapshot and a Genesis event referencing it, replay from the retained snapshot and suffix, and an all-workspaces immediate transaction under a new authority incarnation. Quiesce before admitting effects, atomically suppress unstarted rows, reconcile kept effects without losing their bindings, and quarantine unmappable work with a reason. The converter still refuses several cases outright, including unmapped stages and migration of active script decisions. The fixture replay and sanitized state test exercise Rust values. Crash/restart, outbox reconciliation and the full store rehearsal still need integration coverage. [Design §§10.1–10.3](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#101-how-work-in-flight-migrates), [field decisions](../../crates/workflow/src/migrate/fields.rs#L16-L62), [migration rehearsal limits](../ov-1281/README.md#acceptance).
2. **Switch runtime execution and commands.** Implement the `AdapterExec` side over the existing coding handlers, including durable operation identity, timer/PR observation sources, stop and settlement with proof, and fenced admission and resource handling. Feed host/workspace occupancy into the reducer. Replace legacy action and configuration translation with station-based commands and named adapters. Keep the shipped `get` contract through a projection while consumers move. Before switching, reconcile present-day behavior including followers, script decisions, retained run/failure history, admission, ticket/group lifecycle, and old bundles. Some fields currently survive only in the legacy log. [Execution contract](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#32-what-an-adapter-must-implement), [cutover step 3](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#10-cutover), [current field decisions](../../crates/workflow/src/migrate/fields.rs#L16-L62).
3. **Finish the authoring and process-change contract.** Implement and persist the preview with revision and digest checks and resolve the open migration cases, preserving settlement ownership at replaced stations. Close pure-core gaps for followers, unresolved settlement intent, group deciders, authorization and input bounds as required for production. Wire stations, held/retired work and previews into the apps and manager skill, then retire the compatibility projection. Section 10 separately calls for board continuation links and puts the real command, assistant and content integrations after cutover. [Preview](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#75-who-writes-the-mapping-and-how-a-change-is-previewed), [open questions](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#76-open-migration-questions), [core implementation gaps](../ov-1266/README.md#where-the-code-departs-from-the-design-and-why), [steps 4–6](../../docs/engine-pipeline.md#10-cutover).

**Recommendation: finish the coding cutover as an explicit engine project.** The owner direction calls for workspace-wide workflow changes that take effect for work in flight and a core usable beyond coding. The paired reducers and migration fixtures give that plan a starting point. Scope the first delivery to the four coding layouts and make retained effects, refusal/quarantine cases, publication in flight and replay from persisted Genesis the cutover acceptance tests. If the owner is retiring those product goals, choose the deletion option and record that decision on the board.
